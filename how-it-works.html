How it works

A system you can inspect, not a black box.

Ablak does not ask a language model what a clerk probably meant. It matches what was heard against a written, reviewable set of counter phrases, and it refuses when it is not sure.

Three steps

01

Listen

Capture Hungarian speech through the browser's speech recognition (hu-HU), or accept typed text. Typing works in any browser.

02

Match

Compare the recognised request with a structured knowledge base of what officials, bank clerks, doctors and shop staff actually say.

03

Guide

Return an actionable instruction: what is asked, what to do, which document to hand over, and a Hungarian reply. Or refuse when confidence is not enough.

Why a knowledge base?

Someone at a counter needs an instruction, not a translation: which document do I take out of the folder? A general model can phrase an answer fluently while being wrong about a document or a procedure, and a confident wrong instruction gets in the way of the help clerks often try to give: the clerk thinks you understood. So the answers live in a written knowledge base that a person can read and check.

How a card is organised

  • The Hungarian phrase, plus other ways the same thing is really said (840 forms in total).
  • Keywords that identify the move, the counter it belongs to, and a risk level.
  • What it means, what to do, which document to hand over, and a Hungarian reply, written in both English and Russian.

How matching works

Two signals are combined. Character 4-gram similarity survives Hungarian suffixes and recognition noise. IDF-weighted keyword stems stop boilerplate openings such as "Kérem" from dominating. Choosing the counter first narrows the search, which makes near-identical phrases from other places stop competing.

Why the model must not invent documents

An optional AI fallback exists for phrases outside the knowledge base. It is off by default, its answers are always labelled unverified, and it is never allowed to name a document.

Confidence and refusal

The matcher has three outcomes, each with its own look and its own label. Refusing is a designed result, not an error.

High confidence

It answers

Score of 0.52 or more and a clear gap to the next best phrase. Meaning, action, document and a Hungarian reply are shown.

Medium confidence

It asks you to check

Score between 0.30 and 0.52, or two phrases score almost the same. It shows its best guess with alternatives and a caution.

Low confidence

It refuses

Below 0.30 it gives no guess. It offers a Hungarian sentence asking the clerk to repeat more slowly.

A case we got wrong first

During testing, the line "Kérem a személyi igazolványát és a TAJ-kártyáját" (your ID card and your TAJ card) was matched with confidence to the passport card, because "személyi igazolvány" was the strongest signal. The user would have been told to hand over a passport and missed half the request. The fix was a dedicated card for that combined request, and a test that now checks every phrase resolves to its own card.

Lowering the thresholds would raise how often it answers, and that is exactly the wrong trade here.
